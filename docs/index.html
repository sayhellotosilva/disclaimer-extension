The disclaimer AI companies don’t write.

A free Chrome extension that marks AI tools on any web page and shows what’s behind them: the data centres, the minerals, the workers, the water, your data and the politics.

Download for Chrome Code & sources on GitHub

v1.1 · free and open source · sends nothing about you · updates itself · Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc

disclaimer on a social media post. Every name with an asterisk opens a card.

Why

AI is designed to feel weightless: a text box, an answer, nothing else.

Behind every prompt there is a building on farmland, a river cooling it, minerals mined in Congo and China, people in Nairobi and Manila labelling data, and a company lobbying the people who write the rules. None of it shows up in the interface.

Most of us sit in the middle: not the people who own these systems, not the people who pay the highest price for them, but the people who use them every day because work expects it. We’re not powerless. We’re mostly uninformed. disclaimer puts the missing footnote right where you read about AI, so you can decide what to do with it.

What a card shows

Where it livesThe data centres and chips it runs on.
What it’s made ofThe minerals in those chips, and where they’re mined.
Who trains itThe workers and outsourcing firms behind it.
EnvironmentEnergy, water and emissions.
Terms and conditionsWhat happens to your data, and what you can switch off.
PoliticsWhat it spends lobbying the EU.
disclaimerIts biggest documented controversy.

Covers ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, Meta AI, Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Le Chat, Midjourney and Figma Weave. Model names lead to their maker: “Opus 5.5” opens Claude, “GPT-5” opens ChatGPT.

Install in two minutes

  1. Download the zip and unzip it.
  2. In Chrome, go to chrome://extensions.
  3. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  4. Click Load unpacked and choose the disclaimer-extension folder.
  5. Pin the orange asterisk, then open any page that talks about AI.

Checked in October 2026. Found something wrong or out of date? Tell me on GitHub.

Privacy

disclaimer reads the text of the pages you visit on your own computer, only to find AI names. It never sends that text, or anything about you, anywhere: no server, no account, no tracking. The only thing it downloads is the public card data from GitHub, so a correction reaches everyone without reinstalling.

Limits

  • It’s a curation. Choosing the categories and the facts is itself a way of classifying the world. Every choice is sourced so you can check it.
  • Transparency can look worse than silence. A company that publishes its water use can look dirtier than one that publishes nothing. “Not disclosed” is a finding, not a clean record.
  • Facts change. Data centres open, lawsuits end. The code is open so it can be corrected.
  • It was built with AI. I made it with Claude, which has its own card.
